================================================================================
 A TREE OVER GB SHAPES: STRATEGY FOR ENUMERATING NON-FLAT BOUNDARY MESOSTATES
================================================================================
 Recorded 2026-09-07.  Context: the sigma5 [001](-2 1 0) Cu sweep in
 examples/GbFacetMeshing, at the point where the engagement label was found to
 describe the enumeration rather than the boundary and was relabelled.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  THE PROPOSAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Choose a GB *shape* first, by picking points in the volume around the boundary.
For that shape, find the coincidence points that lie on it -- a bunch of leaves
on a branch.  The engagement pattern of those points then gives the states
belonging to that shape, and to that shape alone.

Explore the shapes as a tree:

    depth 0   the flat boundary; enumerate all of its states
    depth 1   pick one point away from the flat surface, making a tetrahedron;
              the collection of such point choices is the first branch, each
              with its own leaves
    depth 2   take those tetrahedra and pick another point away from each,
              and so on

The state count blows up with depth, so restrict the search to points that
lead to the formation of low index planes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  WHY THE CORE MOVE IS RIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

As the code stands, the engaged nodes *determine* the shape: the faceted
surface is triangulated through the coincidence points, so shape and engagement
are one variable.  The enumeration therefore cannot distinguish

    same shape, different engagement       from       different shape

and every question about faceting is entangled with a question about which
sites are used.  Separating the two is the correct factorization, and doing it
incrementally -- flat, then one point off-plane, then two -- is well posed.

The ordering is also a good curriculum, because it is monotone in a physical
parameter (corrugation) and can be stopped at any depth with a complete
statement: "all mesostates with up to N off-plane nodes".  That is a
publishable claim in a way that "all mesostates we happened to reach" is not.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  THREE THINGS THAT WILL BITE, IN ORDER OF SEVERITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1  "STATES BELONGING TO THAT SHAPE ALONE" IS FALSE UNLESS ENFORCED
     ---------------------------------------------------------------

This is the relabelling bug wearing a different hat, and it sits at the root of
the tree.

Adding one off-plane point makes the shapes *nested*, not disjoint: the flat
shape is the degenerate case of the tetrahedron with zero off-plane
displacement.  Worse, the displacement field cannot be aimed at the engaged
nodes alone -- wherever it happens to close the gap between two other atoms of
the two grains, those meet as well -- so a "tetrahedron" state can realise
exactly the flat state's coincidence set and be the same boundary under another
name.  The code already knows this shape of problem; the maxEngaged directory
comment notes that "a limit of 4 contains everything a limit of 3 does".

Measured on the flat sweep, the fraction of states realising more coincidences
than they engaged:

        engaged   sampled   realised more   largest excess
           1         24       18  (75%)          +4
           2         60       17  (28%)          +3
           3         60        5  ( 8%)          +2
           4         60        2  ( 3%)          +1
         5..18       60        0                  --

Rare overall, but overwhelming at exactly the depth the tree starts from.

THE FIX that makes the levels genuinely disjoint is the predicate now in
countCoincidences(): at depth d, keep only states in which every off-plane node
is engaged AND realises a coincidence.  Without it, whole subtrees are spent
re-deriving the parent's states.


3.2  SYMMETRY CANONICALIZATION IS WORTH MORE THAN THE LOW-INDEX FILTER
     -----------------------------------------------------------------

The flat sweep's symmetry families run to 24 identical copies (the `copies`
column of the level-2 rank-1 state), and its 24 singly-engaged states collapse
to 14 distinct relaxed structures.  Those factors MULTIPLY down the tree: an
uncanonicalized frontier carries every symmetry image of every shape and
expands each one separately.

Canonicalize each point set under the CSL point group together with the
in-plane translations before it enters the frontier, and deduplicate on that
canonical form.  This is the highest-value addition and belongs BEFORE the
low-index filter, because it prunes the frontier rather than the leaves.


3.3  THE LOW-INDEX RESTRICTION NEEDS TWO CORRECTIONS
     -----------------------------------------------

Right instinct -- low-index facets are what real faceted boundaries are made
of -- but:

  (a) A facet's plane is set by a TRIPLE of nodes, not by the new point.  The
      criterion is therefore a constraint on the point relative to its
      triangulation neighbours, which is good news: that is incrementally
      checkable, exactly as the tree wants.  But how many triangles a new node
      creates depends on the in-plane triangulation, so "pick a point and get a
      tetrahedron" only holds where that node has exactly three neighbours; in
      general it is a cone over however many triangles surround it.  The facet
      count per step is a property of the mesh, not of the point.

  (b) MORE IMPORTANT: a plane that is low index in grain A is generally high
      index in grain B, because the grains are misoriented.  Screening on one
      lattice admits facets that are implausible in the other.  Screen on the
      CSL indices, or require low index in both lattices.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.  HOW THE COMBINATORICS PAN OUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Shapes at depth d go like C(P, d), with P the number of admissible off-plane
points per cell, times the engagement enumeration on each shape's own site set.
The flat run fixes the scale of that second factor: 24 candidate sites gave
921599 clash-free states.  So the cost is

        C(P, d)  x  (states per shape)

and P is the one number that decides whether depth 3 is reachable.

  ==> MEASURE P FIRST, before building any of this: count the admissible
      off-plane points in the intended slab at the intended dMax and
      minSiteSeparation.

      P a few dozen  -> depth 2 comfortable, depth 3 needs the symmetry quotient
      P in hundreds  -> depth 2 already needs symmetry AND a beam


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.  RECOMMENDATION: EXHAUSTIVE TO DEPTH 2, BEAM BEYOND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Make the deep levels a beam search rather than an exhaustive walk: keep the
best B canonical shapes by energy at each depth and expand only those.  GB
energy is roughly additive over facets plus junction terms, so a shape whose
sub-shape is already poor rarely becomes good.  Not rigorous -- the failure
mode is a bad intermediate that turns good -- but that is cheap to hedge with a
wider beam near the root and a few random restarts.

Drawing the line at depth 2 keeps the exact claim ("all mesostates with up to
two off-plane nodes") while treating the deeper levels honestly as a search
rather than an enumeration.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.  ONE PIECE IS SMALLER THAN IT LOOKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Find the coincidence points on that shape" is a restriction of machinery that
already exists.  The Sites search takes candidates from a slab about the flat
plane via slabHalfThickness in include/Lattices/GbShifts.h; what is needed is
that same predicate with distance-to-slab swapped for distance-to-surface.

Be precise about the object, though: those candidates are (xA, xB, uA, uB)
pairs that the field BRINGS together within dMax -- not CSL points that
coincide on their own.  The leaves of a branch are the former, which is both
the right thing for the mesostate machinery and a larger set than the latter.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.  A RESIDUAL PROBLEM NO LABELLING SCHEME CAN FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Relabelling by realised coincidences describes the DEFORMED structure honestly,
but distinct deformed structures can relax onto one minimum.  In the flat run's
24 singly-engaged states there are only 14 distinct relaxed structures, and the
ground state at 7.36761356 eV is reached by 11 of them carrying two different
realised labels (4 and 5).  States 004 and 019 genuinely differ before
relaxation -- 004 has a 4-atom coincidence site, 019 a 3-atom site plus an
extra pair -- and still relax together.

The only remedy is a structural deduplication on the minimized structures,
canonicalized under the CSL symmetry and the in-plane translations.  Whatever
the tree does about shapes, it will need this at the leaves.

================================================================================
 8.  MEASUREMENTS AGAINST SECTIONS 3.2 AND 4
================================================================================
 Recorded 2026-09-11.  Everything below is measured on sigma5 (310) [001] Cu at
 slabHalfThickness 0.40, the faceted setting, unless a line says otherwise.
 Sections 1 to 7 are left as written; this section only adds numbers to them,
 and where a number disagrees with the text above it says so.

 The instrument is siteSymmetries() and canonicalSiteSet() in
 examples/GbFacetMeshing/GbFacetMeshing.cpp.  They are REPORT-ONLY: nothing is
 wired into the enumeration, for the reason in 8.3.

 siteSymmetries does not derive the group, it finds it.  Every one of the eight
 axis-aligned sign flips is combined with every translation that could carry
 site 0 onto some site, and each of the 8n hypotheses is applied to the whole
 site set and kept only if it reproduces it.  Complete over that pool.  What it
 returns is distinct PERMUTATIONS, so its size is the order of the image of the
 group in S_n; operations that move no site collapse together.  Validated first
 in Python against known-answer sets (a symmetric 2x2 grid in a cell: order 4,
 group-closed; one point nudged: order 1).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  P, WHICH SECTION 4 ASKS FOR FIRST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 879 raw coincidence nodes are not the candidate set.  symmetricDisplacements
Only drops 822 of them, leaving 57, of which 21 are in-plane (the count at
slabHalfThickness 0.0).  So

        P = 57 - 21 = 36 admissible off-plane points          [axisScaling 3]

which is section 4's "a few dozen" case: depth 2 comfortable, depth 3 needing
the symmetry quotient.  Section 5's recommendation stands on measured ground.

Beware the intermediate counts when reading a run's header: 879 nodes, 57
candidates, 51 distinct site positions and 33 distinct projected coincidence
points are four different things, and only 57 is the enumeration's alphabet.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2  THE SITE SYMMETRY GROUP SCALES WITH THE SUPERCELL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  axisScaling   sites   |G|   depth 1        depth 2          depth 3
      1          17      4    17 ->   8      136 ->   46      680 ->  200
      2          34      8    34 ->   8      561 ->   94     5984 ->  820
      3          51     12    51 ->   8     1275 ->  133    20825 -> 1862

  reduction      --      --   x2.1/4.2/6.4   x3.0/6.0/9.6    x3.4/7.3/11.2

Two things fall out.

  |G| = 4 * axisScaling exactly, and sites = 17 * axisScaling, so DEPTH 1 HAS
  EIGHT ORBITS WHATEVER THE SUPERCELL.  The extra symmetry a larger cell brings
  is the translations that cell manufactured; it is not physics.

  THE FACTORS DO NOT MULTIPLY DOWN THE TREE, contrary to 3.2.  The reduction is
  bounded by |G| and approaches it from below -- 2.125, 2.957, 3.400 at a1 --
  because a generic subset has trivial stabiliser and so an orbit of exactly
  |G|.  The saving saturates at |G|; it cannot compound.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.3  BUT THE SYMMETRY IS NOT REALISED AS STATE EQUIVALENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The group order predicts the duplication a sweep should contain.  It is not
there.  Grouping states on (nodes, fused, corrugation, density, unrelaxed,
tethered, spring):

  axisScaling 1, EXHAUSTIVE      271 states ->   231 distinct      1.173x
     family sizes 198x1, 29x2, 1x3, 3x4 -- three families of the predicted four
  axisScaling 3, 2,917,988 states -> 2,879,908 distinct            1.010x
     97.4% singletons, largest family 3

Against a predicted 4x and 12x.  The a1 figure is exhaustive, so this is not a
sampling artefact, and 1.01x reproduces independently the 1.07x ceiling that
section 6.1 of performanceVsOldFormulation.txt measured by another route.

THE CAUSE IS THE ENTANGLEMENT SECTION 2 NAMES.  A candidate is not a point, it
is (xA, xB, uA, uB).  siteSymmetries verifies only that an operation permutes
the POSITIONS; the displacement data attached to them does not generally map
across.  Some of the eight sign flips almost certainly swap the grains, which
is no symmetry of a bicrystal whose grains are misoriented.

CONSEQUENCE FOR 3.2, AND IT IS NOT A MISSED OPTIMISATION.  Canonicalising the
frontier on site positions would have merged about three quarters of genuinely
distinct states -- deleted physics, leaving a smaller table that still looks
entirely plausible.  This is why the code above reports and does not act.  3.2
should go to the bottom of the list until something accounts for the gap, and
the symmetry worth having is one acting on the whole of (xA, xB, uA, uB).

The one real factor anyone has observed remains 7's: 24 singly-engaged states
collapsing to 14 distinct relaxed structures.  That is a structural dedup at
the leaves, on minimised configurations, not a canonicalisation at the frontier
-- a different mechanism from 3.2, and the one to build.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.4  A SECOND, INDEPENDENT ARGUMENT FOR THE CORE MOVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section 2 argues for choosing the shape first on the grounds that it is the
right factorization.  It is also what makes the field computation scale, which
is a separate argument and a measured one.

Because the faceted surface is triangulated THROUGH the engaged points, every
state is its own shape and brings its own triangulation.  Measured over the
first 160k states of the faceted sweep, the pool of distinct triangles grows

        triangles = 18.7 * states^0.90          about 5.3 new per state

against 2,144 triangles for the WHOLE 26,879-state flat sweep, 0.08 per state:
sixty-six times the per-state growth.  Two consequences, both bad:

  * 39 million triangles and some 4 GB by the end of a 10.7M-state run.
  * GbFacet::packKey gives the triangle identity a 24-bit field, so the pool
    overruns it at 4.16 million states -- 38.9% of that sweep -- and because
    the survey catches per state, EVERY REMAINING STATE IS SILENTLY TALLIED AS
    A FAILURE.  The sweep reports "done" having surveyed 39% of the ensemble.
    (Now bounded: triangleInterner is capped, and an uninterned triangle falls
    back to the direct sum, which is the same number.  OILAB_FACET_TRIANGLE_
    CAPACITY moves the cap.)

Under the tree the states of a branch share one surface and therefore one
triangulation, so the pool becomes shapes x triangles-per-shape: of order 13,000
at depth 2 against 39 million.  The memo's premise -- bounded pool, unbounded
states -- holds again.  So the core move is not only better posed, it is the
difference between the field cost scaling and not.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.5  WHAT IS NOT THE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recorded so nobody spends time here.  The faceted sweep decays badly in
throughput -- 53 states/s average falling to 25 over 15 hours before the fixes,
and still decaying after them.  The memo is not the cause:

  * hit rate is 99.9% and RISING, not falling
  * the table never saturates: 2.5M entries at 160k states, against a 4M cap
  * insert contention is 0.1% of lookups
  * pointInterner is static at ~715 entries and grows no further

Nor is the capacity worth tuning.  At equal elapsed time, caps of 2M, 4M and
20M give 324.6, 320.7 and 322.3 states/s -- indistinguishable, and 4M and 20M
reached byte-identical entry counts because neither ever binds.  Only a cap
small enough to bind costs anything: 500k holds the table to 35 MB, drops the
hit rate to 72%, and loses about 6%.  The "table outgrows L3" hypothesis is
unsupported at these sizes.

The decay is still unexplained.
